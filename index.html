Progress review

Is the plan working?

Never conclude an intervention “didn't work” until you can verify enough data, real implementation, and real access. Answer four checks.

The five fidelity checks
QuestionVerifyIf it's weak
Was it delivered?Sessions or supports actually happened as planned.Fix scheduling, staffing, or access before judging effectiveness.
Was the dosage reached?Minutes, frequency, group size, and duration were adequate.Correct dosage before intensifying content.
Was it delivered as designed?Critical components and routines were present.Provide coaching, modeling, or implementation support.
Did the scholar have access?Attendance, schedule, transportation, or behavior didn't block participation.Remove the access barrier and re-establish exposure.
Was progress measured?Enough valid data points to see a trend.Collect the missing evidence before a major decision.

Meetings

A meeting is for one decision

The meeting coordinates the work; it isn't the intervention. Make sure the right team owns it, bring the evidence, and leave with something someone can carry out.

1

Is this the right team?

Start with the team that controls the condition. Move up only to add authority or capacity.

2

Bring six things. Leave with six things.

Bring

  1. A clear need: observable and measurable, not a label.
  2. Current evidence: outcome data and relevant observations.
  3. Tier 1 context: what core support is already in place.
  4. What's been tried: with implementation details, not just a list.
  5. Scholar and family voice: what helps, what gets in the way.
  6. The decision needed today.

Leave with

  1. A precise problem or root-cause statement.
  2. A matched intervention or system action.
  3. A named lead owner and partner roles.
  4. Frequency, duration, and start date.
  5. A progress measure and a fidelity check.
  6. A review date and a decision rule.

If the team can't name all six, the plan isn't ready to implement yet.

3

Record the decision

A record of the decision, not a transcript of the meeting. Saved in this browser only. Use initials, not full names.

      The Guide

      The thinking behind the tools. Read the part you need.

        One system, four connected areas

        MTSS in GRPS is one system for academics, attendance, behavior/PBIS, and SEL/mental health. Every scholar gets strong Tier 1. Additional support is layered on by need, and a scholar can receive different levels of support in different areas at the same time.

        Academics

        Core instruction, screening, differentiation, targeted intervention, progress monitoring, intensification.

        Behavior / PBIS

        Schoolwide expectations, restorative and preventive practice, targeted supports, individual problem solving.

        Attendance

        Prevention, early identification, root-cause inquiry, barrier removal, engagement, individual plans.

        SEL / Mental health

        Belonging, explicit SEL instruction, targeted support, coordination, qualified intensive services.

        Foundational conditionsTeam-based leadership · Family and community partnership · Data-based decision making · Continuous improvement
        Michigan's five essential components
        1. Team-based leadership: clear authority, roles, meeting structures, and barrier removal.
        2. Tiered delivery system: Tier 1 for all, with more targeted and intensive support layered by need.
        3. Selection and implementation of supports: evidence-based practices matched to the defined need and implemented with fidelity.
        4. Comprehensive screening and assessment: universal, diagnostic, progress, and fidelity evidence, each used for a specific decision.
        5. Continuous data-based decision making: outcomes, fidelity, and experience data decide what happens next.

        The eight rules

        If you remember nothing else, remember these.

        1. Tiers describe the support, not the scholar.A tier is a level of intensity, not a label or a placement. Tier 3 is not special education, and special education is not “Tier 4.”
        2. Tier 1 never stops.Targeted and intensive supports are layered on top of strong core instruction and conditions, never in place of them.
        3. When many struggle, fix the core first.A pattern across a class, grade, or subgroup is a system question. Don't turn a Tier 1 problem into dozens of Tier 2 referrals.
        4. Start where the problem can be solved.MTSS is not a referral ladder. The team that owns the condition owns the first response. Moving up should add authority or capacity, not duplicate the case.
        5. An action is not an intervention.A call, letter, referral, meeting, or handoff documents what staff did. It's part of an intervention only when it is designed to change the barrier, and the team checks whether it did.
        6. Check implementation before judging impact.Before deciding a support didn't work, confirm it was delivered, at the planned dosage, and that the scholar actually had access to it.
        7. Every meeting ends with one documented decision.Owner, dosage, measures, review date, and decision rule, or the plan isn't ready yet.
        8. Families are partners, not recipients of notices.Ask before deciding. Don't make families retell the same story to five people.

        Don't wait for a formal meeting to begin reasonable Tier 1 support or remove an obvious access barrier. The meeting exists to improve problem solving, not to delay help.

        From need to decision

        The goal isn't to move scholars through tiers. It's to solve a defined problem with the least intensive support likely to work, implement it well, and let evidence decide what happens next.

        1. NoticeIdentify the pattern
        2. UnderstandDefine the need and why
        3. MatchSelect a fitting support
        4. PlanOwner, dosage, goal
        5. ImplementDeliver as designed
        6. MonitorOutcome and fidelity
        7. DecideContinue, change, fade

        Start at the earliest step that isn't clear yet. If the team can't say what's happening, for whom, where, and under what conditions, it isn't ready to match a support.

        Who solves what

        Use the lowest level of the system that can effectively solve the problem. A need doesn't have to travel through every level, and building or district teams can act directly when data show a system problem.

        Level 1

        Classroom, grade, or content team

        Strengthens Tier 1 instruction, routines, relationships, and differentiation. Notices patterns and defines concerns.

        Level 2

        Scholar Success Team

        Solves defined scholar or small-group problems that need coordination. Sets owner, dosage, monitoring, review, and decision rule.

        Level 3

        Building MTSS Leadership

        Owns building-wide implementation: Tier 1 health, subgroup patterns, intervention capacity, schedules, staffing.

        Level 4

        District MTSS Support

        Sets the framework, tools, and standards; builds capacity; reviews cross-school patterns; removes district barriers.

        Connect to another level when…

        • The team lacks authority or resources to remove the barrier.
        • The same pattern appears across classrooms, grades, or schools.
        • Intervention capacity or scheduling prevents adequate implementation.
        • Several departments or services must coordinate.
        • Fidelity problems reflect a system condition, not one case.

        Don't escalate just because…

        • A scholar scored below benchmark once.
        • Someone wants another person to “take over.”
        • A parent contact or referral already happened.
        • The team hasn't defined the need yet.
        • The intervention wasn't implemented long enough or well enough.

        Entering, intensifying, fading, exiting

        DecisionEvidence requiredWhat the team does
        Enter Tier 2Need is defined; Tier 1 access verified; data show supplemental support is needed.Select a matched intervention with dosage, owner, goal, measure, and review date.
        ContinueProgress trend adequate, fidelity strong.Continue through the planned duration. Don't change a working intervention early.
        Repair fidelityProgress inadequate, implementation inconsistent.Fix scheduling, staffing, training, or dosage before calling it ineffective.
        IntensifyProgress inadequate with strong fidelity and access.Smaller group, more time or frequency, stronger match, an added component, or Tier 3 problem solving.
        Fade or exitGoal met; performance stable and generalizing.Reduce support deliberately while Tier 1 continues and maintenance is monitored.
        Tier 3Need is significant, persistent, complex, or not responding to well-implemented Tier 2.Individualized problem solving, more frequent monitoring, coordinated supports, documented response.

        Tier 3 does not automatically mean special education. Evaluation decisions follow legal criteria and comprehensive evidence, not a sequence of failed tiers.

        When intensifying, change one or two things deliberately (more opportunities to respond and feedback, a smaller group, more explicit instruction) on the same validated platform, add a phase line, and review the response to that change before changing anything else.

        Start with the question, not the test

        Assessment helps only when the team knows what decision it's making. Use the smallest amount of evidence that answers the question reliably.

        EvidenceAnswersDon't use it for
        Universal screeningWhere are strengths and needs? Who may need more? Is Tier 1 enough?A complete diagnosis from one score.
        Diagnostic / root causeWhat exactly is the skill gap, barrier, or function?Delaying support while collecting unneeded data.
        Progress monitoringIs the scholar responding at the expected rate?Tracking outcomes unrelated to the target skill or barrier.
        Fidelity / implementationWas the support actually delivered as designed?Punitive evaluation of staff.
        Data review cadence

        Universal / benchmark

        Fall, winter, spring. Are enough scholars succeeding with Tier 1? Where are patterns concentrated? Strengthen the core before building individual plans.

        Targeted / intensive

        On the intervention cycle, more often for intensive plans. Is support reaching the right scholars at the planned dosage and producing the expected progress?

        A benchmark like “80% meeting expectations” is a useful system-health signal, not a rigid referral cutoff. Use GRPS-adopted expectations alongside subgroup patterns, fidelity, and access.

        Families are partners in problem solving

        Family engagement should deepen the team's understanding of the scholar's strengths, experience, and barriers. It shouldn't be reduced to notifying families after decisions are made.

        Before the plan

        • Share the need in clear, nontechnical language.
        • Ask what the family and scholar are noticing.
        • Learn strengths, routines that work, and cultural and language considerations.
        • Explain what the school already does at Tier 1.

        During and at review

        • Explain the intervention, goal, owner, and review date.
        • Agree on how and how often to communicate.
        • Ask whether the support is improving the scholar's actual experience.
        • Discuss continue, modify, or fade decisions before finalizing them.

        Communicate in the family's preferred language. A family contact isn't automatically an intervention; it becomes one only when it directly helps change the barrier.

        MTSS looks different across grade spans

        PK–5

        Foundational literacy and numeracy, classroom routines and SEL, attendance habits, family partnership, early screening, and quick skill-specific support. Progress measures should track the taught skill.

        6–8

        Course performance, the move to multiple teachers, belonging, attendance by period, behavior across settings, and early-warning indicators. Coordinate so scholars aren't overloaded with disconnected interventions.

        9–12

        Attendance, course failures, credits and on-track status, engagement, graduation trajectory, and EWIMS early warning. Plans account for schedule, credit recovery, postsecondary relevance, and scholar voice.

        A secondary scholar can be on track in one indicator and at serious risk in another. Look across attendance, engagement, and course performance together.

        Classroom teacherDaily Tier 1 access and evidenceDifferentiates core instruction, notices patterns, documents what was tried and how the scholar responded, implements agreed supports, communicates with families.
        MTSS Scholar Success TeacherTargeted intervention and monitoringDelivers supplemental academic, behavioral, or SEL support; progress monitors; keeps intervention records; raises fidelity or capacity concerns. Role description ↗

        Start with grade-level core. Add intensity with purpose.

        Tier 1 is the grade-level core for every scholar. Tier 2 supplements it and stays aligned to it. Tier 3 adds intensity through time, focus, frequency, or group size, and often uses the same resource as Tier 2 under more intensive conditions.

        A curriculum feature, digital program, or scaffold is not an MTSS intervention on its own. It becomes one when the team defines the target, dosage, owner, progress measure, fidelity evidence, review date, and decision rule.

        Seven essential ingredients of Tier 1

        1. Culturally affirming instruction
        2. Standards-aligned learning targets, unpacked, shared, and assessed with scholars
        3. Scholar artifacts that show modeled thinking and ongoing learning
        4. Classroom routines, norms, and expectations
        5. Gradual release: I do, we do, you do
        6. Timely checks for understanding
        7. Scholar talk and discourse norms

        What good looks like

        SituationWeak responseImplementation-ready response
        Chronically absentSend an attendance letter and refer to FSS.Family inquiry identifies unreliable transportation. FSS coordinates a morning plan for 20 school days; the team tracks completed rides and weekly attendance and reviews at four weeks.
        Well below reading benchmarkPlace in reading intervention.Diagnostic shows decoding is the gap. Explicit decoding intervention 4 × 30 min/week, group of 4, six weeks; weekly decoding probe and fidelity check guide the decision.
        Disruption during independent workBehavior referral and a parent call.Team finds task avoidance tied to a skill gap. Teacher adjusts task entry and teaches a help-seeking routine; work completion and replacement behavior are monitored.
        Many in one grade strugglingRefer many scholars to Tier 2.Grade team reviews core instruction, common assessment items, pacing, and fidelity; strengthens Tier 1, then identifies who still needs more.
        Example: attendance barrier-removal plan
        Concern
        Attended 74% of the last 20 school days.
        Evidence
        Four of five recent absences fell on days the family's usual ride was unavailable; family interview confirms.
        Intervention
        Reliable morning transportation plus a backup plan for 20 school days.
        Owner
        FSS coordinates; family confirms changes; attendance contact reviews weekly.
        Monitor
        Days present, planned rides completed, family report on the barrier.
        Decision
        Fade when access is stable and attendance improves; modify if rides aren't happening; look for other causes if access is stable but attendance stays low.
        Example: Tier 2 reading plan
        Need
        Diagnostic evidence shows a decoding deficit, not a general “low reading” concern.
        Goal
        Meet the decoding progress target by the six-week review.
        Dosage
        4 × 30 min/week, group of four, six weeks.
        Monitor
        Weekly skill-aligned probe plus intervention attendance and fidelity.
        Decision
        Continue if response is adequate; repair fidelity or access first if weak; intensify deliberately only when both are adequate and progress isn't.
        Example: a strong SST decision
        Problem
        Completes fewer than 30% of independent multi-step math tasks.
        Hypothesis
        Prerequisite computation gaps; disruption happens mainly after difficult tasks are assigned.
        Decision
        Targeted prerequisite instruction, adjusted task entry points, and an explicitly taught help-seeking routine.
        Owners
        Interventionist provides skill support; teacher implements task adjustments and the routine.
        Evidence
        Skill probe, task completion, replacement behavior, exposure, fidelity.
        Example: a Tier 1 system response
        Pattern
        A large share of Grade 6 scholars are failing the same math standard.
        Wrong move
        Dozens of Tier 2 referrals.
        Inquiry
        Common assessment items, instructional opportunity, pacing, prerequisite demands, classroom implementation, subgroup patterns.
        Response
        Strengthen and reteach the core sequence; monitor classwide response; then identify who still needs supplemental support.

        Document the decision, not the activity

        A record should let another team member see what problem was identified, what support was chosen, whether it happened, what changed, and what was decided.

        Minimum record

        • Need or problem statement
        • Root-cause or need hypothesis
        • Intervention and rationale
        • Owner and partners
        • Start date, dosage, duration
        • Progress and fidelity measures
        • Decision and next step

        Avoid overload

        • Don't duplicate the same plan across tools unless required.
        • Don't log every contact as its own intervention.
        • Don't collect information you won't use for a decision.
        • Keep protected information to what the educational purpose needs.
